# Vijay Rentala — LinkedIn Article Strategy (Grounded Version)
## Based on actual experience: AI/ML in financial services, model risk, fair lending, enterprise buying

**The positioning that differentiates you:** You are one of the few people who has built an AI/ML product from zero inside the actual constraints of financial services regulation — SR 11-7, fair lending, explainability requirements, model risk committees — and sold it to enterprise buyers who buy to manage risk, not create value.

That experience is rare. Nobody else is writing from inside that specific window.

---

## The Article Series — "What Building AI for Banks Actually Taught Me"

8 articles. Long-form (1,500–2,500 words). Each grounded in a specific operational experience you've had.

---

### Article 1 — The Core Contrarian Piece (publish first)
**"Every AI Sales Deck I've Seen in Financial Services Is Pitching the Wrong Thing"**

The real insight: Enterprises in regulated industries don't buy AI to create value. They buy it to manage risk — regulatory risk, reputational risk, credit risk, operational risk. Vendors pitch efficiency and ROI. Banks approve things that reduce their exposure. These are different conversations with different decision-makers.

---

### Article 2 — The Model Risk Reality
**"What SR 11-7 Actually Looks Like From the Inside — If You're the Vendor"**

The real insight: Most AI vendors treat model risk management as a compliance step at the end. It's actually the product. What model validation teams look for, what gets models rejected in MRC review, and why the vendors who survive this process build for it from day one.

---

### Article 3 — The Explainability Gap
**"'Explainable AI' Is a Marketing Term. Here's What Regulators Actually Need."**

The real insight: LIME and SHAP make great conference slides. Examiners want audit trails, consistent output documentation, and adverse action notices they can defend. The gap between what vendors call "explainable" and what a bank's model risk team accepts as explainable is enormous — and it kills deals.

---

### Article 4 — The Hard Call
**"The Decision I Had to Make When There Was No Right Answer and No Time to Wait"**

The real insight: Regulatory timelines move unpredictably. At some point you face a decision with genuinely incomplete information — proceed and risk being wrong, or wait and risk missing the window. What actually drives that call, how you make it defensible, and what you learn about your own risk tolerance.

---

### Article 5 — The Champion Problem
**"Your Biggest Threat in an Enterprise AI Deal Is Not the Competition"**

The real insight: The internal champion is the single point of failure in any enterprise deal. When your champion loses internal credibility — due to a reorg, a budget fight, a compliance objection they couldn't field — the deal dies regardless of product quality. The warning signs, what to do when you see them, and why you should never build a single-thread sales motion.

---

### Article 6 — The POC Trap
**"Banks Don't Do POCs to Evaluate You. Here's What They're Actually Doing."**

The real insight: POCs in financial services are often a procurement delay tactic, not a genuine evaluation mechanism. How to distinguish a real evaluation from a deferral, what to insist on before committing to free work, and the contractual and relational moves that separate POCs that convert from POCs that die.

---

### Article 7 — The Fair Lending Problem
**"I Built AI for Lending and Fair Lending Compliance Was Harder Than the Model Itself"**

The real insight: Building an AI model that performs well is the easier half. Making it defensible under Regulation B, ECOA, and HMDA — documenting adverse action reasons when the model is a black box, running disparate impact tests, explaining to an examiner why your feature set doesn't create proxy discrimination — is the harder half that most founders don't see coming.

---

### Article 8 — The Timeline Truth
**"Enterprise AI Timelines Are Not a Procurement Problem. They're an Organizational Metabolism Problem."**

The real insight: The reason AI takes so long inside a bank isn't slow procurement — it's that approval requires alignment across risk, compliance, legal, IT security, and the business line, each with different priorities and different interpretations of the same regulatory guidance. What each stage actually looks like, who controls it, and what you can design around vs. what you have to accept.
